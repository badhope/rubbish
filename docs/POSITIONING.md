# Product and vocabulary

**English · [简体中文](POSITIONING.zh-CN.md)**

## In one line

**Rubbish is a public writing and discussion community: open a question, keep the process, and say the thing plainly.**

The English category is **Rubbish — Public Writing & Discussion**.

## Main entrances

`Home / Library / Collections / Forum / Guide / Me`

- **Home:** one line, a few pieces worth reading first, and discussions happening now.
- **Library:** long-form writing and absurd writing side by side; filter by topic, year, or section.
- **Collections:** one theme, one reading line. Order only, never a ranking.
- **Forum:** methods, retrospectives, team-finding, and life beyond writing. Short threads live here.
- **Guide:** a first walk-through of where things go and where to speak.
- **Me:** work, bookmarks, notifications, experience, and settings.

## Roles

`Reader / Author / Peer Reviewer / Curator / Steward`

- **Reader:** sign up to read, bookmark, comment, and join discussions.
- **Author:** earned by publishing a first piece.
- **Peer Reviewer:** apply as an author; the steward team approves. Can write feedback on work under peer review.
- **Curator:** handles collections, authorship claims, and site notes.
- **Steward:** handles reports, announcements, members, and site operations.

## Vocabulary

- Use **work**, **publish**, **public**, **peer review**, **basic check**, **decision note**, **passed / not passed**.
- Use **collection** for a reading order. Do not use volume, issue, or journal number.
- Use **section** for a forum category. Do not use board.
- Use **topic** for a work's direction. Do not use research field or discipline.
- Use **experience** for member activity. Do not use activity score, XP, or points level.
- Public means the work can be read. The site does not perform academic evaluation or issue institutional proof.

## Copy rules

1. Start from something concrete: a piece, a stuck point, a failed attempt, a question not yet settled.
2. Lead with what people can do. Do not define the site by what it is not.
3. Short sentences, few adjectives. “At ten in the evening the cursor was still blinking” beats “a premium creative ecosystem.”
4. Serious is allowed; absurd is allowed. Fiction, exaggeration, and jokes must be clear in the body.
5. Keep four boundaries: real material can be checked, fiction is labeled, private people stay private, and sources are credited.

## Visual rules

- One idea per screen; everything else waits below the fold.
- The home opens with one line and two actions, not a wall of stats.
- Use lists only where continuous browsing is the point. Index rows and asymmetric sections work better on entry pages.
- Design desktop and mobile separately. Do not shrink a desktop layout into a phone screen.
