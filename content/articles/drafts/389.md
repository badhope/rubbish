Unifying Mass Defects via Instantaneous Mass in Curved Spacetime An Analytic Relation Among Outputs of Numerical Relativity **Abstract** In General Relativity, the absence of global time-translation symmetry implies that mass cannot be treated as a universally conserved quantity simply, while the covariant divergence condition  guarantees strict conservation only at each spacetime point. Based on this fundamental fact, we introduce the concept of instantaneous mass. We emphasize that the formulas of Special Relativity remain strictly valid, provided the analysis is strictly confined to a local tangent space. However, the physical referents of the symbols within these SR formulas must be re-examined to re-identify their physical meanings in curved spacetime. By applying the formulas of Special Relativity within local inertial frames, we derive an exact expression for this instantaneous mass and establish a unified framework for describing mass-defect phenomena. This framework subsumes gravitational binding energy, nuclear binding energy, and annihilation energy — seemingly distinct mechanisms — under a single relational expression, which reduces to their respective forms in different limits. Without introducing any free parameter, our computation for the final mass of the GW150914 binary black hole merger gives a post-merger mass of 62.16 , which agrees closely with the reported final mass of 62.3 . The present work is not a substitute for numerical relativity simulations; the required input quantities (rest masses and relative speed) still rely on numerical relativity at the current stage. Nevertheless, it may reveal analytic relations among some of the outputs of numerical relativity.

1. **Introduction**

In Special Relativity [1], the flat Minkowski spacetime possesses a global time-translation symmetry. By Noether's theorem, this symmetry implies the conservation of energy and momentum—a cornerstone of modern physics that has been confirmed to high precision in countless experiments within local, approximately inertial frames.

General Relativity [2], however, presents a radically different picture. In a curved, dynamical spacetime, time-translation symmetry is generically broken. Consequently, there is no global conservation law for mass-energy in the universe at large. The question of whether energy is globally conserved in general relativity has recently been revisited by Aoki [3], who highlighted the profound difficulties arising from the absence of a global timelike Killing vector. The celebrated equation ▽μTμv=0 —the vanishing covariant divergence of the energy-momentum tensor—does not imply the existence of a conserved global energy [4]. It merely enforces a local, differential conservation that holds pointwise, but cannot be integrated to a finite region without additional symmetries (such as a timelike Killing vector field), which our expanding universe precisely lacks.

This tension between the global non-conservation mandated by General Relativity and the local conservation inherited from Special Relativity has long been recognized, [5] yet its physical consequences remain underexplored. If mass-energy is not strictly conserved on cosmological scales, how does this non-conservation manifest in finite regions of spacetime? Is it merely a negligible artifact at laboratory scales, or does it leave an imprint on the dynamics of matter itself?

In this paper, we attempt to address these questions by proposing a thermodynamic-kinematic relation that captures the effect of the absence of global conservation in a local and instantaneous sense. Rather than treating mass as a globally conserved quantity, we propose a complementary approach: defining mass as an instantaneous dynamical quantity in the local tangent space. This allows us to derive an exact local relation, which naturally resolves the mass defect phenomena and yields a quantifiable framework for mass-energy evolution. Our starting point is the observation that, while global conservation fails, instantaneous conservation still strictly holds in any infinitesimal time interval. This idea, combined with the relativistic formula, yields a relation between mass and velocity that has been conjectured [6], but is here derived from first principles.

A central methodological premise of this work is that all discussions are conducted within the curved spacetime background described by General Relativity. Consequently, the conclusions of Special Relativity—which describe flat spacetime—are not universally applicable. Before invoking any SR relation, one must first ascertain whether the situation under consideration genuinely qualifies as the local frame demanded by the equivalence principle—with explicit justification, not merely by assertion. Such an invocation must then be accompanied by a rigorous scrutiny of the relation’s applicability boundaries and the physical meaning of each symbol involved. The conditions and reasons for their use must be explicitly specified. More importantly, the crux lies not in the symbols themselves, but in the physical concepts they denote in curved spacetime—mechanically transplanting the original notation without contextual scrutiny is unwarranted. Through this methodological approach, we uncover a natural bridge between the local validity of Special Relativity and the global non-conservation inherent in General Relativity. The present work does not propose a modification of General Relativity, but rather an interpretation: it aims to make explicit the thermodynamic "cost" and the kinematic consequences of the absence of global conservation laws in curved spacetime.

The work is motivated by a simple observation: in a curved spacetime without global time-translation symmetry, mass-energy is not globally conserved. [3] This raises a question: why not conceive of mass as an instantaneous quantity, and see what happens if we re-derive the fundamental laws of physics from this premise? By exploiting the local strict conservation enforced by , we apply the formulas of Special Relativity within local frames and derive an exact expression for the instantaneous mass as measured by a local observer. This expression provides a unified description of mass‑defect mechanisms ranging from gravitational binding energy to annihilation energy. Remarkably, without introducing any free parameter, our computation for the final mass of the GW150914 binary black hole merger is in close agreement with the value reported by LIGO.

Many contemporary approaches attempt to reconcile theoretical predictions with observations by modifying the gravitational field equations or introducing additional degrees of freedom. The present work takes a different stance. It suggests that General Relativity itself may be essentially complete; the long-standing difficulties might arise not from a deficiency in the theory, but from an insufficiently scrutinized conception of mass. All derivations in this paper draw solely upon the fundamental formulas of relativity, without introducing any new parameters, correction factors, or higher-order terms. Rather than modifying the equations themselves, we propose a revised understanding of the physical concepts their symbols denote. By re-examining the physical meaning of mass—particularly its instantaneous nature in curved spacetime—this approach yields a different physical picture and may provide a unified resolution to many seemingly disparate problems without altering the underlying framework of General Relativity. Special Relativity itself is not wrong; rather, an error might have occurred in the process of substituting the symbols.

We maintain that any modification to the fundamental formulas lacks sufficient justification. Without altering a single fundamental formula, a re-examination of what the symbols physically denote, followed by an algebraic re-derivation, can yield entirely different conclusions. Due to this re-identification of what the symbols physically denote, the derived formulas obtained through algebraic derivation may superficially appear different from the traditional derived formulas. However, it is crucial to recognize that the underlying fundamental formulas remain entirely unchanged and are strictly adhered to.Attempting to re-identify the physical referents of the symbols in fundamental formulas—rather than modifying the formulas themselves (e.g., by adding higher-order terms or coefficients of unclear origin)—should be recognized as a legitimate approach to physics research.

**5. Discussion and Summary** The preceding sections have placed side by side two distinct expressions for kinetic energy. The standard formula  is derived from the Lorentz transformations of Special Relativity, which presuppose a globally flat Minkowski spacetime with time-translation symmetry. In such a spacetime, Noether's theorem guarantees conserved energy, and the intrinsic mass is an invariant—constant for all observers.

The instantaneous formula , by contrast, emerges from a different starting point: we do not assume global conservation. Instead, we only require that in any infinitesimal interval, the local laws of Special Relativity hold strictly. This is precisely the logic of General Relativity: at each point, spacetime is a flat tangent space, but globally, curvature and the absence of Killing vectors break the conservation laws.

Thus, the two kinetic energies reflect two different conceptions of spacetime:
* belongs to the idealized tangent space—the flat geometry that locally approximates the real curved spacetime.
* belongs to the actual curved spacetime, where mass-energy is not globally conserved, and matters evolve irreversibly as they move.

The difference between the two kinetic expressions has already been interpreted thermodynamically as . But it can also be viewed geometrically: it represents the cumulative effect of spacetime curvature on the motion of matter. In a perfectly flat spacetime, the standard formula would hold exactly for all velocities, and no such difference would appear. The very existence of a non-zero difference signals that matter is not moving in a flat, globally symmetric background, but in a curved spacetime where global conservation fails.

In this sense, the quantity  can be thought of as a geometric entropy—a measure of the information lost about the object's state due to the curvature of spacetime. It is the price paid for the fact that the intrinsic mass is not an absolute invariant but a relational quantity that evolves with its motion through the curved geometry.

This interpretation resonates deeply with the philosophical underpinnings of General Relativity. The equivalence principle tells us that at any point, we can erect a local inertial frame where physics is that of Special Relativity. But these local frames are only instantaneous approximations; they cannot be extended globally. Our derivation makes this limitation explicit. The logarithmic term   quantifies the failure of the global flat-spacetime description. It is the integrated effect of spacetime curvature on the energy-momentum relation.

We have now arrived at a remarkable triple identity: The triple identity is the central result of this work. It unifies three perspectives:
* Kinematic: the logarithmic term arises purely from the relativistic relation between velocity and energy, when mass is treated as instantaneous.
* Thermodynamic: it represents the entropy generated during acceleration, linking kinematics to the arrow of time.
* Geometric: it quantifies the failure of the global flat-spacetime description, i.e., the cumulative effect of spacetime curvature on the energy-momentum relation.

This triple nature echoes the deep interconnections uncovered in black hole thermodynamics and Jacobson's derivation of Einstein's equations from the Clausius relation. [12] It suggests that irreversibility is not an add-on to General Relativity, but a feature already latent in its local structure, waiting to be made explicit by a proper identification of thermodynamic variables. In this unified picture, the motion in curved spacetime is not a reversible, conservative process, but an irreversible thermodynamic evolution in which the decrease of mass is accompanied by an increase in entropy. The Lorentz factor  serves as the bridge between kinematics, thermodynamics, and geometry—its logarithm appears both as the entropy generated and as the discrepancy between local and global descriptions of the motion.

**Appendix E: Internal Conceptual Inconsistency in the Standard Framework** It might be objected that high-precision tests of mass-energy conservation in particle physics contradict the notion of a velocity-dependent inertial mass. We argue that this objection rests on an internal conceptual inconsistency inherent in the standard framework itself.

Consider any exothermic nuclear reaction. If the objector insists that the intrinsic mass of a specific physical object is an immutable constant, then the observed mass defect—the fact that the sum of the rest masses of the products is less than that of the reactants—becomes inexplicable. To account for the released energy, one must concede that the observed kinetic energy release is derived at the expense of the rest mass of the system.

If the objector then shifts ground and claims that the mass defect is merely a manifestation of the conservation of total mass-energy, they face a different problem: the conserved quantity is no longer the rest mass  of the isolated system, but a generalized energy that changes its form. In other words, the very quantity whose conservation is invoked is no longer the static label  but a dynamical quantity that decreases in the process. At this juncture, the standard framework inadvertently performs a conceptual substitution: while continuing to speak of "rest mass," it has in fact replaced the notion with a variable that behaves exactly like the instantaneous mass of the present theory.
